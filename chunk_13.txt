A 3-hour competitive badminton session represents an alternating aerobic-anaerobic interval regime characterized by short, explosive bouts of maximal power output (vertical jumps, lunges, violent racket smashes lasting 2 to 6 seconds) interspersed with brief recovery intervals of 10 to 20 seconds. During active rallies, the skeletal muscle relies almost exclusively on the anaerobic phosphagen system (ATP-PCr) and rapid glycolysis to fuel high-velocity cross-bridge cycling in Type IIx fast-twitch muscle fibers. The total metabolic energy expenditure across a 3-hour matchplay block frequently exceeds 1,800 kilocalories. When combined with baseline basal metabolic rate (BMR) and daily non-exercise physical activity, a 16-year-old competitive athlete with a low body mass index experiences a daily caloric burn pushing 3,500 to 4,000 kilocalories. 7.2 Glycogen Depletion, Blood Shunting, and the "Hollow Stomach" Phenomenon Sustained competitive exertion under high heat and humidity triggers profound physiological adaptations. As active muscle contractions devour available circulating glucose, the endocrine system elevates glucagon, epinephrine, and cortisol secretion, commanding the liver to break down its glycogen reserves via glycogenolysis. Once hepatic and muscle glycogen stores drop below a critical threshold, the body experiences acute systemic depletion. Concurrently, to support thermoregulation and oxygen delivery to working limbs, the sympathetic nervous system induces massive peripheral vasodilation while executing splanchnic vasoconstriction—diverting up to 80 percent of blood flow away from the gastrointestinal tract and visceral organs toward the active skeletal muscles and skin.
