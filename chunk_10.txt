Conversely, if chunks are too large (e.g., 2,000 words), retrieval precision drops, irrelevancies flood the prompt, and the model suffers from the "Lost in the Middle" phenomenon, where transformer attention mechanisms overlook critical details buried in long contexts. The industry standard baseline for technical documentation utilizes chunk sizes ranging between 250 and 350 words, paired with a sliding window overlap of 10 to 15 percent (25 to 50 words). The overlap ensures that sentences crossing arbitrary split boundaries remain semantically coherent in at least one indexed block. 5.4 Lexical Search (BM25) versus Dense Semantic Embeddings Information retrieval within RAG architectures operates along two distinct paradigms: lexical keyword matching and dense vector semantic search. Lexical algorithms, such as Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) and the advanced BM25 (Best Matching 25) algorithm, evaluate the exact lexical overlap between query terms and document chunks. BM25 incorporates term frequency saturation and document length normalization, making it exceptionally fast, computationally lightweight, and immune to semantic drift when searching for exact part numbers (such as "INMP441" or "NEMA 17"), hexadecimal memory addresses, or specific error codes. However, BM25 fails completely when queries utilize synonyms or conceptual descriptions that share zero vocabulary with the source text. Dense semantic embeddings solve this by passing text through neural encoders (such as OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` or BGE-M3) that output dense numerical arrays (e.g., 1,536 floating-point values). The semantic similarity between the query vector $\vec{Q}$ and document vector $\vec{D}$ is calculated using the Cosine Similarity metric: $$\text{Cosine Similarity}(\vec{Q}, \vec{D}) = \frac{\vec{Q} \cdot \vec{D}}{\|\vec{Q}\| \|\vec{D}\|} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Q_i D_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Q_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} D_i^2}}$$ Advanced enterprise architectures combine both paradigms into **Hybrid Search**, running parallel BM25 and vector queries before re-ranking results using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). 5.5 Autonomous Agentic Loops and Function Calling While standard RAG is passive (retrieve once, generate once), modern AI systems require autonomous, multi-step problem solving.
