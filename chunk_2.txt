In deep sleep mode, power to the primary RISC-V core, high-frequency oscillators, and wireless basebands is completely severed. Wake-up triggers are delegated exclusively to the Real-Time Clock (RTC) controller, which monitors specific general-purpose input/output (GPIO) pins, such as GPIO 2 or GPIO 9, via internal weak pull-up resistors. When an external active-low tactile button press pulls the monitored pin to ground, the RTC power domain signals the internal power management unit to restore the main core rail within 350 microseconds, allowing rapid event processing before resuming sub-50-microampere dormancy. 1.3 Acoustic Sensing via I2S and Digital MEMS Microphones Analog electret microphone capsules are fundamentally unsuited for high-fidelity edge transcription due to electromagnetic interference, power supply ripple, and signal degradation across breadboard jumper runs. The INMP441 digital micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) microphone resolves these challenges by integrating an acoustic sensor, an analog-to-digital converter, and a standard Inter-IC Sound (I2S) serial interface onto a single silicon die. The INMP441 outputs 24-bit pulse-code modulation (PCM) digital audio directly into the ESP32-C3's internal direct memory access (DMA) controller, bypassing processor execution cycles entirely during raw stream ingestion. The physical bus consists of three mandatory signal lines: the Serial Clock (SCK or BCLK), which synchronizes bit-level transmissions; the Word Select line (WS or LRCLK), which delineates left and right audio channels; and the Serial Data line (SD), which streams incoming bits. By tying the hardware L/R channel selection pin to ground, the INMP441 transmits audio data exclusively in the left frame of the 32-bit clock packet. In high-performance speech-to-text pipelines, the sampling rate is locked to 16,000 Hertz (16 kHz) with 16-bit effective resolution, matching the acoustic training distribution of OpenAI Whisper and Google Gemini speech engines.
