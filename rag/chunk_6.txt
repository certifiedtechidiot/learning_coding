These motors feature two independent internal phase coils that must be energized sequentially in alternating polarities. The Allegro A4988 microstepping bipolar stepper driver serves as the translator between high-level logic pulses from the microcontroller and high-current inductive loads. The driver integrates an internal DMOS translator that interprets two digital logic lines: STEP and DIRECTION. Logic transitions on the STEP pin advance the internal state machine, driving current through the motor windings via internal H-bridge circuits operating under a fixed off-time pulse-width modulated (PWM) current-chopping regime. 3.3 Microstepping Resolution and Current Limit Calibration Driving stepper motors in full-step mode produces severe acoustic resonance, mechanical vibration, and positioning jitter that manifests as waviness in handwritten lines. The A4988 driver provides three digital microstepping selection inputs: MS1, MS2, and MS3. Pulling all three selection pins high activates sixteenth-step microstepping mode (1/16th step), dividing each 1.8-degree mechanical step into 16 discrete sub-steps. This elevates the system resolution to 3,200 microsteps per motor revolution. At a standard 20-tooth GT2 timing pulley pitch (2 millimeters per tooth, yielding 40 millimeters of belt translation per full revolution), the linear motion resolution achieves an exceptional 12.5 microns per microstep. To prevent motor phase overheating and missed step errors, the driver's current-limiting reference voltage ($V_{\text{ref}}$) must be physically calibrated using the onboard trim potentiometer according to the formula: $$I_{\text{limit}} = \frac{V_{\text{ref}}}{8 \times R_s}$$ Where $R_s$ represents the resistance of the onboard current sense resistors (typically 0.1 ohm or 0.05 ohm).
