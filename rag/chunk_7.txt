Because full stepper-driven lead screws add excessive mass to the moving carriage, miniature drafting systems utilize lightweight RC servomotors, specifically the TowerPro MG90S metal-gear micro servo. The servo utilizes an internal potentiometer and feedback circuit to hold position based on the duty cycle of a 50-Hertz PWM control signal (typically varying between 1.0 millisecond for 0 degrees down to 2.0 milliseconds for 180 degrees). In CoreXY architectures, pen-lift vibration frequently stems from inductive electrical noise coupling into the servo signal lead from adjacent stepper phase wires. Installing a dedicated 100-microfarad electrolytic decoupling capacitor directly across the servo's 5-volt power and ground rails, combined with physical software timers that detach the servo pulse train once movement completes, completely eliminates holding jitter and prevents ink blotting on delicate paper media. --- CHAPTER 4: AUTOMATED VISION, OCR & REVOLUTIONARY EVALUATION SYSTEMS 4.1 The Architecture of Rubrica: Automated Document Ingestion The digitization, analysis, and pedagogical evaluation of handwritten student examinations has historically formed the primary operational bottleneck in educational institutions. The Rubrica platform operates as an autonomous, closed-loop software and hardware pipeline designed to ingest physical handwritten answer sheets, extract semantic mathematical reasoning, and output verified grades alongside personalized feedback. The architecture is segregated into three distinct decoupled layers: the Physical Ingestion Layer, the Computer Vision Extraction Layer, and the Deterministic Reasoning Layer. In high-throughput institutional deployments, physical papers are ingested via CoreXY optical flatbed scanners or overhead camera gantries utilizing fixed focal length prime lenses to eliminate barrel distortion and perspective warping. 4.2 Image Preprocessing, Binarization, and Deskewing Pipelines Raw image capture of physical student answer scripts is fraught with real-world artifacts: non-uniform classroom ambient lighting, shadows cast by mobile phone cameras, page curvature, and rotational skew. The Computer Vision extraction pipeline processes incoming images through an automated spatial normalization pipeline. The first stage executes edge detection using the Canny algorithm after applying a 5x5 Gaussian kernel filter to suppress high-frequency sensor noise.
