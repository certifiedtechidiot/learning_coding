Wireless frames are subdivided into three fundamental classes: Data Frames, which transport upper-layer IP packets; Control Frames, which manage channel contention (such as Request-to-Send and Clear-to-Send); and Management Frames, which negotiate network topology, authentication, and client association. Unlike wired Ethernet where physical cable isolation provides a natural perimeter, wireless transmissions propagate isotropically through physical space, exposing the network interface to arbitrary monitoring and packet injection. Crucially, in legacy WPA and WPA2-Personal networks, management frames are transmitted in unencrypted plaintext, creating severe structural vulnerabilities in network integrity. 6.2 Deauthentication Frame Injection Mechanics The Deauthentication frame is an 802.11 management subtype utilized by access points (APs) and client stations to gracefully terminate an active wireless link. Because management frames in standard 802.11 networks lack cryptographic signatures or sequence authentication, any radio transmitter operating in monitor mode can forge a deauthentication packet.
