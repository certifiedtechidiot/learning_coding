The ESP32 silicon examines the logic levels of specific GPIO pins during the initial power-on reset (POR) cycle to configure internal operating parameters, such as flash memory voltage, boot source, and UART logging mode. GPIO 12 (MTDI) serves as the primary flash voltage strap. If an external peripheral pulls GPIO 12 to a high logic level during power-up, the ESP32 internally shifts its internal flash operating voltage from 3.3 volts down to 1.8 volts. This mismatch causes catastrophic flash read errors, locking the microcontroller in a permanent bootloop displaying `flash read err, 1000`. Similarly, GPIO 0 and GPIO 2 govern the download boot mode versus SPI flash execution mode. All peripheral CS lines, pull-up circuits, and display control inputs must be thoroughly audited against these strapping behaviors to ensure reliable power cycling in autonomous installations. --- CHAPTER 3: COREXY KINEMATICS, STEPPER MOTOR DYNAMICS & CNC DRAFTING 3.1 Cartesian Mechanics versus CoreXY Kinematics Traditional Cartesian desktop CNC plotters assign dedicated motors to distinct Cartesian axes: one motor moves the toolhead along the X-axis gantry, while a secondary motor translates the entire gantry along the Y-axis. This topology introduces severe mechanical drawbacks; the Y-axis motor must carry the full physical weight of the X-axis motor, causing rotational inertia, motor resonance, and axis acceleration limits. The CoreXY kinematic system resolves this inertia penalty by fixing both primary stepper motors (Motor A and Motor B) permanently to the stationary structural frame of the machine. The toolhead carriage is linked to these motors via a continuous, interlaced single-plane or stacked-plane synchronous timing belt arrangement.
